## The Landscape Has Changed

Five years ago, the tech career playbook was straightforward: learn to code, get a job at a big company, climb the ladder. That playbook still works, but the game has fundamentally changed.

### Skills That Actually Matter

Technical skills get you in the door. But what keeps you relevant:

- **Systems thinking** — understanding how pieces fit together
- **Communication** — translating complex ideas for different audiences
- **Adaptability** — the willingness to learn new tools quarterly, not yearly
- **Domain expertise** — deep knowledge in a specific area that AI can't easily replicate

### The AI Question

Every tech professional is asking: "Will AI take my job?" The more useful question is: "How do I become someone who uses AI to do work that wasn't possible before?"

The people thriving right now aren't fighting AI — they're building with it.

### Practical Steps

1. **Build in public** — share your work, even if it's imperfect
2. **Network intentionally** — one genuine conversation beats 100 LinkedIn connections
3. **Go deep before going broad** — specialists are valued more than generalists in most contexts
4. **Stay curious** — read outside your field, attend talks outside your expertise

### The Long Game

Careers aren't sprints. The most successful people I know optimized for learning over earning in their first decade. The compound interest of knowledge is real.
